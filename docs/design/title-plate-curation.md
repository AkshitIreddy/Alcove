# Title plates and binder's emblems

Status: **current**. This note refines the flat book vocabulary in
`src/art/spines.ts`, `src/art/covers.ts`, `src/art/bookDesign.ts` and
`src/art/bookSurprise.ts`. It does not supersede the rendering architecture.

## Evidence and ruling

The catalogue follows the physical grammar visible across library and museum
bindings: titles are normally lettered directly, set inside open blind/gilt
fillets, or carried on restrained rectangular leather, paper, or vellum
labels. Shaped medallions are focal emblems, not title badges.

### Synthesis

The sources converge on six useful families: direct blind/gilt/ink lettering;
pasted paper or vellum tickets; rectangular leather lettering pieces; dyed or
inlaid cross-bands; open fillet compartments; and restrained inscription
shoulders. Frames derive from nested fillets, mitred returns, open corners,
horizontal compartments and centre/corner tooling. They do **not** support a
catalogue of oval pills, shields, scallops, fake metal plates, bevelled UI
cards or repeated texture fields. The main disagreement is regional density:
Islamic and Renaissance presentation bindings can be very rich, while
archival and publisher bindings are spare. Alcove resolves that through the
one-focal-programme rule rather than averaging both into every cover.

## Title catalogue

The active catalogue is construction-led and append-only. Persisted retired
ids continue to normalize into a safe active treatment.

| Family | Active constructions |
| --- | --- |
| Direct anchors | blind impression; direct gilt; direct ink |
| Integrated title architecture | open dentelle field; Grolier nested field |
| Paper and skin | laid-paper ticket; vertical deckled-paper slip; vellum shoulder band; vertical parchment slip |
| Leather lettering pieces | low morocco single rule; broad morocco double rule; Grolier leather lozenge; oval calf compartment; inset two-tone leather centre |
| Library cloth | compact buckram press label |
| Structural material fields | upper-third half-leather field; gilt-ruled crossband; vertical cloth inlay; split-board field |
| Open tooling | Oxford blind compartment; Arts & Crafts blind title; perimeter title bridge; armorial pediment field; six-lobed Islamic cartouche; Renaissance quadrilobe |

The production catalogue has **twenty-five** treatments. Every active id maps
one-to-one to a `CoverTitleFurniture` construction and has an individual
cover layout; variation includes placement, orientation, scale and material,
not merely line count or colour. New ids are appended to `TITLE_PLATES`;
historical ids are never reordered. Surprise Me additionally observes
material eligibility: calf/morocco pieces belong on leather or split
bindings, dyed bands on cloth/leather/split bindings, and paper/vellum slips
on paper, vellum or split archive families.

### Rejected variants during rendered comparison

- The inherited fifteen-option catalogue was rejected as the baseline: too
  many warning bands, floating web cards and selection-box compartments.
- First 25-option cut was rejected because direct, paper, leather, crossband
  and line-field rows collapsed into five repeated silhouettes at shelf size.
- Second cut introduced architectural families but was rejected for dentelle
  X-ends, sideways sticky-note slips, cramped lozenge/oval lettering, a
  floating inset panel, barcode-dense perimeter fillets and undersized lobed
  fields.
- The final pass made vertical furniture physically tall, assigned every
  treatment an eligible material specimen, removed contrast-driven fake
  fields, separated the two morocco hierarchies, and enlarged the armorial,
  lobed and quadrilobe title loci with explicit text clearance.

## Cover frame catalogue

The existing twelve accepted frames remain unchanged. Six append-only authored
constructions add generation variety: Mitred Calf Panel, Open Dentelle,
Grolier Lozenge Panel, Library Triple Mitre, Renaissance Open Panel and Blind
Acanthus Panel. Each has a distinct rule/corner/turn signature; none uses dots,
studs, rings, ticks, blur, lighting or patterned ground.

## Emblem catalogue

An emblem must read as one distinct silhouette on both the native cover and a
resting shelf spine. The approved vocabulary favours broad stars, palmettes,
arabesques, rosettes, fleurs, leaves, formal sprays and a few unmistakable
heraldic tools. It uses one ink, an open construction, and at most two accents.

The late botanical tail keeps or redraws acanthus spear, carnation, iris,
poppy seedhead, olive and honeysuckle sprays, lotus palmette, rowan, primrose,
dog rose, reed bundle, moresque knot, and Tudor rose. The artichoke, strawberry,
grape, samara, willow, columbine and cedar-cone ids normalize to broader oak,
laurel or tulip tools because their identifying detail disappeared at shelf
size.

## Composition hierarchy

One book gets one leading decorative programme:

1. An authored material field owns the cover, or
2. an architectural frame owns it, or
3. one matched cover-and-spine emblem owns it, or
4. the title treatment owns it.

A shaped/architectural title field never competes with a large emblem and an
ornate frame. Emblem-led books receive a quiet direct, ticket, or band title
and a quiet frame. Frame-led books receive a quiet title and no emblem.
Title-led books receive no emblem. Patterned or already-figured materials are
quieter still.

## Verification contract

Curating names is not enough. Render:

- every active title at native 142 x 197 cover pixels and enlarged detail;
- every title again on an eligible material-matched cover and at 60 x 83 shelf
  pixels, so a blue specimen board cannot hide material or scale failure;
- every active emblem on the native cover and the resting shelf spine;
- an adversarial board combining the strongest title/frame/emblem inputs;
- a deterministic negative control that deliberately admits retired shaped
  plates or focal stacking and must make the gate fail.

Inspect the images. Unit tests additionally pin append-only normalization,
one-to-one furniture mappings, cache-key separation, and Surprise hierarchy.
The specimen gate hashes the twenty-five shelf renders and fails on an injected
duplicate; the adversarial gate separately injects an Oxford statement field,
architectural frame and large emblem and must report `GATE ALIVE`.
